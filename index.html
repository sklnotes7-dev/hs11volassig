What is Special Olympics?

The mission statement on their website is as follows:

Special Olympics delivers world-class sport programs to people with an intellectual disability. Our programs cater to all ages and a wide range of abilities.

Special Olympics NB offers three programs for individuals aged 2-88

Active Start/FUNdamentals

Active Start is a program for aged 2-6, and FUNdamentals for 7-12. Quoted from the official website, "These programs provide youth with opportunities to engage in active play and develop fundamental motor skills."

Sports

From the website, "Special Olympics New Brunswick offers both winter and summer sports for athletes at a level that best suits their athletic and individual ability ages 13 and up." They operate in ten regions and offer eighteen different sports.

Unified Sport

According to the website, this is a new program developed in partnership with the NB Physical Education Society, New Brunswick Interscholastic Athletic Association.

The History of Special Olympics

The history of special Olympics dates back to the 1950's and 60's with Eunice Kennedy Shriver, who saw the unfair treatment of individuals with intellectual disabilities and took action.

How do they support people?

Sports

According to the website, "Special Olympics offers 30-plus Olympic-style individual and team sports that provide meaningful training and competition opportunities for persons with intellectual disabilities." They also have progrzms duvh as Young Athletes, an early childhood play program for ages 2-7 for children both with and without intellectual disability.

Unified Schools

From the site, "Unified Schools begin with Special Olympics Unified Sports® to build inclusive school communities for young people of all abilities. This programming creates an environment where everyone feels welcome, valued, and included both in school and in their local community." The goal of Unified Schools is to reduce the stigma around intellectual disability. Some of the impact it has had includes:

  • Reduce Bullying - 94% of schools reported that Unified Schools made a big impact in reducing bullying, teasing, and offensive language.
  • Change Attitudes and Behaviors - Through Unified Schools, over 2 out of 3 students without intellectual disabilities (ID) experienced substantial positive change in interactions with their peers with ID.
  • Build Community - In Kenya and India, 4 out of 5 teachers reported an improved sense of community after participating in Unified Schools.
